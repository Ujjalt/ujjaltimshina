<h3><strong>The Ladder That Leans Against Nothing</strong></h3>

<p>
They told me<br>
knowledge is a ladder,<br>
and I believed them<br>
for a long time.
</p>

<p>
Every rung<br>
a paper,<br>
a degree,<br>
a citation,<br>
a name spoken<br>
in rooms<br>
I was not invited to.
</p>

<p>
And I climbed.<br>
Quietly at first,<br>
then louder,<br>
because I learned<br>
that quiet<br>
is mistaken<br>
for empty.
</p>

<p>
The higher I went,<br>
the more I noticed<br>
the hands.
</p>

<p>
Some were<br>
holding me up.<br>
Most were<br>
holding someone down—<br>
a foot,<br>
a shoulder,<br>
a throat.
</p>

<p>
And the strange thing<br>
is this:
</p>

<p>
the ones who pushed<br>
the hardest<br>
were praised<br>
for their confidence.
</p>

<p>
The ones who spoke<br>
the loudest<br>
were given<br>
the most room<br>
to speak.
</p>

<p>
The ones who stepped<br>
on others<br>
were called<br>
leaders.
</p>

<p>
I stood there<br>
with my questions,<br>
soft as dust,<br>
and watched<br>
the hierarchy<br>
breathe.
</p>

<p>
It breathes<br>
by taking air<br>
from someone else's lungs.
</p>

<p>
It grows<br>
by keeping<br>
other things<br>
small.
</p>

<p>
And I thought—<br>
this is not<br>
a garden.
</p>

<p>
This is<br>
a forest<br>
where every tree<br>
is measuring<br>
its shadow<br>
against the next.
</p>

<p>
The pigeons<br>
outside my window<br>
do not do this.
</p>

<p>
They rise<br>
without a rank.<br>
They turn<br>
without a title.<br>
They fold the sky<br>
into shapes<br>
no one owns.
</p>

<p>
And I wonder<br>
if knowledge<br>
was ever meant<br>
to be a ladder at all—
</p>

<p>
or if it was meant<br>
to be a sky.
</p>

<p>
Something we enter<br>
together,<br>
not something<br>
we climb<br>
alone.
</p>

<p>
But the world<br>
has chosen<br>
its architecture.
</p>

<p>
And I am still here,<br>
standing at the bottom<br>
of a ladder<br>
that leans<br>
against nothing,
</p>

<p>
wondering<br>
if I should climb,<br>
or if I should<br>
simply<br>
walk away.
</p>

<p>
&mdash; Ujjal Timshina
