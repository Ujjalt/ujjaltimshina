<p><em>
It is August 1, 2026. It is 11:08 AM. Like most Saturdays, the mathematics lab is almost empty. I have always loved these quiet weekends—not because I dislike people or wish to avoid conversations, but because silence has a way of slowing the world down. On days like this, the race outside seems to disappear for a while, leaving only thoughts, questions, and the quiet companionship of mathematics.
</em></p>

<h3><strong>The Silence of a Saturday</strong></h3>

<p>
There is something comforting<br>
about an almost empty department.<br>
The corridors become quiet,<br>
the doors remain closed,<br>
and time itself seems to walk<br>
a little more slowly.
</p>

<p>
I enjoy being here<br>
on Saturdays and Sundays.<br>
Not because I dislike people,<br>
not because I hate my colleagues,<br>
nor because they disturb me.<br>
Silence simply lets me hear<br>
my own thoughts more clearly.
</p>

<p>
Sometimes I feel<br>
as though I am the only<br>
living creature in the world,<br>
trying to understand<br>
a few small moments<br>
without judgement,<br>
without comparison,<br>
without the endless race for survival.
</p>

<p>
Seventeen days ago,<br>
my supervisor gave me a problem.<br>
The truth is,<br>
I had already been trying<br>
to understand the same idea<br>
for months.
</p>

<p>
I am still stuck.
</p>

<p>
That sentence sounds painful,<br>
yet it is perhaps one of<br>
the most common sentences<br>
in mathematics.
</p>

<p>
We become confused.<br>
We build misconceptions.<br>
We make mistakes.<br>
We erase them.<br>
We begin again.<br>
Sometimes progress arrives,<br>
sometimes it does not.
</p>

<p>
Mathematics follows<br>
its own clock.<br>
Life does not.
</p>

<p>
Deadlines continue.<br>
Expectations remain.<br>
The race for survival<br>
never pauses<br>
simply because<br>
an idea refuses<br>
to become clear.
</p>

<p>
Sometimes I wonder<br>
whether everyone<br>
experiences this feeling.
</p>

<p>
Then I speak<br>
to a few colleagues.
</p>

<p>
They smile and say,<br>
"<em>That happens to me too.</em>"
</p>

<p>
Those few words<br>
carry more comfort<br>
than they probably know.
</p>

<p>
Perhaps confusion<br>
is not the opposite<br>
of mathematics.
</p>

<p>
Perhaps confusion<br>
is where mathematics<br>
quietly begins.
</p>

<p>
And so,<br>
on another silent Saturday,<br>
I return to the same notebook,<br>
the same unfinished calculation,<br>
and the same unanswered question—<br>
still hoping that one more attempt<br>
will reveal something<br>
I could not see yesterday.
</p>
