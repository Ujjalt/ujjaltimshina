<div style="background:#0e2e0e; padding:18px 22px; border-radius:8px; color:#e8f5e8; font-family:Georgia, serif; font-size:16px; margin-bottom:20px;">

  A child asks, “Why is the sky so far?” We smile at the question and call it curiosity. We admire the courage of a child who does not yet know which questions are supposed to be asked.

  Years later, we ask questions too, but now they come with fear. “Is this useful?” “Has this been done before?” “Will it get published?” “Will anyone care?” Slowly, we learn to be careful about what we ask.

</div>

<div style="background:#0e2e0e; padding:18px 22px; border-radius:8px; color:#e8f5e8; font-family:Georgia, serif; font-size:22px; font-weight:bold; margin-bottom:20px; text-align:center;">
  Before the Question Dies
</div>

<div style="background:#0e2e0e; padding:18px 22px; border-radius:8px; color:#e8f5e8; font-family:Georgia, serif; font-size:16px; margin-bottom:20px;">

  We stop asking the questions that might make us look foolish. We hide strange ideas before someone calls them stupid. We judge our thoughts before giving them enough time to breathe.

  Some ideas are too simple. Some are too strange. Some are too ambitious. Some are not practical enough. And slowly, we become very good at knowing what not to ask.

  Perhaps that is how curiosity dies. Not with a loud “No,” but with a quiet, “That's a silly question.”

</div>

<div style="background:#0e2e0e; padding:18px 22px; border-radius:8px; color:#e8f5e8; font-family:Georgia, serif; font-size:16px; margin-bottom:20px;">

  I often wonder how many discoveries began as embarrassing thoughts. How many beautiful ideas were abandoned because their creator was afraid of being judged.

  We remember the theorem, the paper, the discovery. We rarely remember the strange little question that came first.

  Perhaps an idea does not need to be right when it is born. Perhaps it only needs a little space to grow.

</div>

<div style="background:#0e2e0e; padding:18px 22px; border-radius:8px; color:#e8f5e8; font-family:Georgia, serif; font-size:16px; margin-bottom:20px;">

  So let the child ask. Let the student wonder. Let the researcher be wrong. Let someone say something nobody understands the first time.

  Not every question needs an immediate answer. Not every thought needs to become a paper. Sometimes the value of a question is simply that it keeps the mind awake.

  Before judging a question, perhaps we should allow it to finish being asked.

</div>

<div style="background:#0e2e0e; padding:18px 22px; border-radius:8px; color:#e8f5e8; font-family:Georgia, serif; font-size:16px; margin-bottom:20px;">

  Because sometimes, the question that everyone laughs at today becomes the doorway to something we were never wise enough to imagine yesterday.

  Curiosity deserves a little courage. Ideas deserve a little patience. And questions, especially the strange ones, deserve a little more time before they are allowed to die.

</div>
