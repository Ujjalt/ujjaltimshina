<div style="background:#0e2e0e; padding:18px 22px; border-radius:8px; color:#e8f5e8; font-family:Georgia, serif; font-size:16px; margin-bottom:20px;">

  A child asks,

  “Why is the sky so far?”

  We smile at the question.

  We call it curiosity.

  We call it innocence.

  We admire the courage of a child who does not yet know which questions are supposed to be asked.

</div>

<div style="background:#0e2e0e; padding:18px 22px; border-radius:8px; color:#e8f5e8; font-family:Georgia, serif; font-size:16px; margin-bottom:20px;">

  Years later, we ask questions too.

  But now we ask them in different rooms.

  We have better clothes.

  Degrees hang on the wall.

  There are papers behind our names.

  There are titles before our names.

  And sometimes, there is fear in our voice.

  The question is no longer simply,

  “Why?”

  It becomes,

  “Is this useful?”

  “Has this already been done?”

  “Will it get published?”

  “Will anyone care?”

  “What if I am wrong?”

  Somewhere along the way, curiosity begins to feel expensive.

</div>

<div style="background:#0e2e0e; padding:18px 22px; border-radius:8px; color:#e8f5e8; font-family:Georgia, serif; font-size:22px; font-weight:bold; margin-bottom:20px; text-align:center;">

  Before the Question Dies

</div>

<div style="background:#0e2e0e; padding:18px 22px; border-radius:8px; color:#e8f5e8; font-family:Georgia, serif; font-size:16px; margin-bottom:20px;">

  So we learn to be careful.

  We stop asking the questions that might make us look foolish.

  We hide strange ideas before someone calls them stupid.

  We judge our thoughts before giving them enough time to breathe.

  Some ideas are too simple.

  Some are too strange.

  Some are too ambitious.

  Some are not practical enough.

  Some do not fit inside the questions that already exist.

  And slowly, without realizing it, we become very good at knowing what not to ask.

</div>

<div style="background:#0e2e0e; padding:18px 22px; border-radius:8px; color:#e8f5e8; font-family:Georgia, serif; font-size:16px; margin-bottom:20px;">

  Perhaps that is how curiosity dies.

  Not with a loud “No.”

  Not with someone standing in front of us and telling us to stop thinking.

  Sometimes it happens much more quietly.

  Someone simply says,

  “That's a silly question.”

  And we remember it.

  We carry that sentence with us.

  The next time an unusual thought appears, we silence it ourselves.

  We become our own critic before anyone else gets the opportunity.

</div>

<div style="background:#0e2e0e; padding:18px 22px; border-radius:8px; color:#e8f5e8; font-family:Georgia, serif; font-size:16px; margin-bottom:20px;">

  I often wonder how many discoveries began as embarrassing thoughts.

  How many beautiful ideas were once dismissed because they sounded strange.

  How many questions disappeared before anyone had the patience to explore them.

  We usually remember the final theorem.

  The published paper.

  The discovery.

  The result.

  We rarely remember the uncertain beginning.

  The moment when someone had only a question and nothing else.

  No proof.

  No evidence.

  No certainty.

  Just a thought that refused to leave.

</div>

<div style="background:#0e2e0e; padding:18px 22px; border-radius:8px; color:#e8f5e8; font-family:Georgia, serif; font-size:16px; margin-bottom:20px;">

  Perhaps an idea does not need to be right when it is born.

  Perhaps it only needs a little space.

  Space to be wrong.

  Space to be incomplete.

  Space to be strange.

  Space to become something that its creator could not yet imagine.

  Mathematics, in particular, has taught me that understanding rarely arrives all at once.

  Sometimes an idea appears as a small discomfort.

  A pattern that does not make sense.

  A question that seems too naive.

  A calculation that refuses to behave.

  And sometimes, hidden inside that discomfort, there is something worth following.

</div>

<div style="background:#0e2e0e; padding:18px 22px; border-radius:8px; color:#e8f5e8; font-family:Georgia, serif; font-size:16px; margin-bottom:20px;">

  So let the child ask.

  Let the student wonder.

  Let the researcher be wrong.

  Let someone say something nobody understands the first time.

  Let an idea remain unfinished for a while.

  Not every question needs an immediate answer.

  Not every thought needs to become a paper.

  Not every curiosity needs to justify its existence.

  Sometimes the value of a question is simply that it keeps the mind awake.

</div>

<div style="background:#0e2e0e; padding:18px 22px; border-radius:8px; color:#e8f5e8; font-family:Georgia, serif; font-size:16px; margin-bottom:20px;">

  We live in a world that often asks us to prove the usefulness of everything.

  What will it give us?

  Where will it lead?

  How much will it matter?

  These are reasonable questions.

  But perhaps we should sometimes ask another one first.

  What if we simply explored it?

  Before asking whether an idea is useful, perhaps we should give it enough time to become an idea.

  Before judging a question, perhaps we should allow it to finish being asked.

</div>

<div style="background:#0e2e0e; padding:18px 22px; border-radius:8px; color:#e8f5e8; font-family:Georgia, serif; font-size:16px; margin-bottom:20px;">

  Because sometimes,

  the question that everyone laughs at today

  becomes the doorway to something

  we were never wise enough

  to imagine yesterday.

  And perhaps the real tragedy is not asking a foolish question.

  Perhaps it is becoming so afraid of judgment that we stop asking questions at all.

  Curiosity deserves a little courage.

  Ideas deserve a little patience.

  And questions, especially the strange ones, deserve a little more time before they are allowed to die.

</div>
